# Coverage notes (paraphrased)

This is the cleanest "What is X?" article in the competitor set — title literally is the question. Lives under Dropbox Help (DocSend is a Dropbox product since acquisition) and is targeted at someone evaluating or just starting with DocSend.

Sections in order: Overview, "What can I share with DocSend?", "DocSend Viewing Experience", "What else can I do with DocSend?". The pattern is: define the product in one paragraph, then break the definition apart into (a) inputs (file types you can share), (b) the recipient experience, (c) advanced capabilities.

Concepts introduced: customizable secure links, viewer analytics (who viewed, time spent per page), supported file types (PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Keynote, plus video/audio/images as download-only), Spaces (deal rooms / virtual data rooms), e-signature, NDA / agreement gating, watermarking, email-domain whitelisting. The article is feature-oriented but framed as "things you can do" rather than a feature list.

Problem framing: traditional email attachments are blind — you don't know if the recipient opened the deck, lingered on a slide, or forwarded it. DocSend is presented as the alternative: secure links plus engagement analytics.

It does NOT explicitly say what DocSend is not. The omission is conspicuous given that DocSend overlaps with cloud storage, e-signature tools, and data rooms.

Reader framing: a business professional sharing sensitive documents — fundraising decks, board materials, sales collateral. Friendly, benefit-led, second-person voice. Estimated 350-450 words. One viewer-toolbar screenshot. No video.
